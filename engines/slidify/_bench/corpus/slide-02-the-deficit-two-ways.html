Public Finance · Two-up

The federal deficit, two ways

In raw dollars the deficit looks unprecedented. As a share of GDP it has been here before — twice in the last forty years. Same metric, two scales, two different stories.

A · Nominal dollars (USD trillions)
The headline number. Each bar is one fiscal year's deficit, not adjusted for inflation or population. The 2020 spike is COVID relief; the 2024 figure of $1.83T is the second-largest on record.
All bars in current dollars. The 2020 figure is the COVID-19 fiscal response.
B · Share of GDP (%)
The economist's number. Same eleven years, same deficits — but now sized against the economy that produced them. The picture flattens. 2024 = 6.3% of GDP, well below the 14.7% peak of 2020 and roughly equal to 1983.
Same eleven years. The crisis collapses to a pulse.
