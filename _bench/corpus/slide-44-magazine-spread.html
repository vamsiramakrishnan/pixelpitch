The Profile P. 42
An interview — in three rooms

What the
quiet hours
are for.

The novelist on slow mornings, the cost of an honest sentence, and learning to leave a draft alone for a year.

The kettle is on for the second time. Halberstam writes longhand until eleven, then walks. Then, she has said in earlier interviews, the day becomes a kind of weather one consents to.

Her new novel The Inland Sea is shorter than the last by half. “I wanted the silence to be load-bearing,” she says. “Not a pause between things — the thing itself.”

We meet in the kitchen, then the study, then a back porch that looks at nothing in particular. In each room she is slightly different, more careful with what she will say.

“A page should cost something,” she offers, stirring nothing in her cup. “Otherwise it isn't a page. It's a sound.”
