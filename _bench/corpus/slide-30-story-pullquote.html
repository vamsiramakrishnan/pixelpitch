Chapter Two · Field notes

The morning we stopped rushing.

It started, as most quiet revolutions do, with a small refusal. We let the kettle whistle a moment longer. We sat where the light was thin, and we noticed it. The day, unhurried, agreed to begin with us, not despite us.

The pace of the work changed the moment we stopped negotiating with the clock. — from the foreword, page 4
